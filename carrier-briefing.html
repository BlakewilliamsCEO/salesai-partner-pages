How the machine actually works

Manufacturers fund demand through the channel, not around it

This is the part most people miss about HVAC. Carrier does not sell to contractors. It sells to distributors, and it subsidizes whatever makes those distributors' dealers sell more equipment. That subsidy is called MDF, and it is enormous.

Step 01 · Manufacturer Carrier

Routes roughly $400M a year in Market Development Funds out to its distributor network.

Step 02 · Distributor Koch Air, Habegger, Carrier Enterprise

~240 of them. Each controls approval for how its dealers may spend those funds — marketing, lead gen, tools.

Step 03 · Dealer The contractor

Spends co-op dollars on approved vendors. More booked jobs pulls more equipment back up the chain.

The unlock is simple to state and hard to earn: once SalesAi is MDF-eligible, a Carrier Factory Authorized Dealer buys it with Carrier's money, not their own. As I understand the program, coverage scales with the dealer's purchase history — from roughly half of it to all of it. That changes the sales conversation from "can you afford this" to "you already have the budget."

It is worth saying plainly for the agency side of this: MDF is not a SalesAi-shaped mechanism. It funds things that move boxes, and demand generation moves boxes. I am working through Carrier's fund guide now to pin down exactly which categories qualify — that answer matters as much to you two as it does to me.

You will never see the Watsco name on a building. Contractors meet it through its subsidiaries — Carrier Enterprise, Gemaire, Baker, East Coast Metal Distributors, TEC, Pierce Phelps, Holman's. What made the call go well

Patrick's problem is the 80/20. Twenty percent of his 150,000 contractors do eighty percent of the purchase volume, and every AI voice company that walks through his door wants access to exactly that twenty percent — the private-equity-backed platforms and the large independents. He is obligated to serve the rest.

"You're the first company I've talked to that is actually targeting that. And honestly, from my perspective, that's where the need is more so than anywhere else."

Patrick Ruhland, Watsco Ventures — 19 August 2026

He named the vendors he has already been pitched: Avoca, Same Day, Broccoli. All of them fishing at the top of the pond. The differentiator he grabbed onto was not our product — it was our willingness to build for the operator going from one million to five.

Why Carrier changes what that partnership is worth

Kinsey — the reason I wanted you to see this rather than just hear about it: the thing you told me in June about Leadgenix, that the potential is in the operators everyone else turns away, is the exact gap Watsco just told me they cannot fill.

  • The long tail is the shared ICP. Carrier's dealer base is 76,450 operators nationally; Watsco's is 150,000. Both organizations are explicitly looking for solutions that reach past the top twenty percent. A Carrier or Watsco dealer at $800K in revenue does not need an AI voice agent first — they need a CRM, a website that converts, and someone running their marketing. That is Leadgenix, and it arrives pre-qualified through a manufacturer relationship rather than paid acquisition.
  • MDF is a subsidy for demand, not just for software. If the approval lands, we will have built a funded path into these dealers. I am still confirming precisely which service categories qualify — but if agency-delivered demand generation is one of them, being alongside us when that door opens is worth considerably more than being introduced to it afterward.
  • September is a room, not a booth. 750 dealer principals in buying posture, away from their phones. Big Leap and Leadgenix are not competitive with each other, and I would rather field both agency teams alongside us than hold the floor alone against Scorpion's incumbency signal.
