## How the machine actually works

### Manufacturers fund demand through the channel, not around it

This is the part most people miss about HVAC. Carrier does not sell to contractors. It sells to distributors, and it subsidizes whatever makes those distributors' dealers sell more equipment. That subsidy is called MDF, and it is enormous.

1. **Manufacturer — Carrier.** Routes roughly $400M a year in Market Development Funds out to its distributor network.
2. **Distributor — Koch Air, Habegger, Carrier Enterprise.** ~240 of them. Each controls approval for how its dealers may spend those funds — marketing, lead gen, tools.
3. **Dealer — the contractor.** Spends co-op dollars on approved vendors. More booked jobs pulls more equipment back up the chain.

The unlock is simple to state and hard to earn: **once SalesAi is MDF-eligible, a Carrier Factory Authorized Dealer buys it with Carrier's money, not their own.** As I understand the program, coverage scales with the dealer's purchase history — from roughly half of it to all of it. That changes the sales conversation from "can you afford this" to "you already have the budget."

It is worth saying plainly for the agency side of this: MDF is not a SalesAi-shaped mechanism. It funds things that move boxes, and demand generation moves boxes. I am working through Carrier's fund guide now to pin down exactly which categories qualify — that answer matters as much to you two as it does to me.

The bet in June was that Ty Cole's introductions would eventually reach past individual distributors into something structural. On 13 August it did. Watsco is not a distributor in the way Koch Air is a distributor.
